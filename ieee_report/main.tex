\begin{abstract}
Face morphing attacks represent a critical vulnerability in Automated Border Control (ABC) e-Gates and electronic Machine Readable Travel Documents (eMRTDs, ICAO Doc 9303). By blending facial features from an unauthorized traveler and a clean-record accomplice into a single composite portrait, a morphed credential can successfully verify against both identities in Commercial-Off-The-Shelf (COTS) face recognition systems. While spatial detectors effectively identify traditional landmark-warped morphs, they exhibit catastrophic failure against generative latent morphs (e.g., StyleGAN2) and degrade severely under analog print-scan conditions. To resolve this vulnerability, this paper introduces \textbf{UID-MAD} (Unified Interpretable Dual-Stream Morph Attack Detection), a single-image framework that explicitly targets the mathematical bifurcation of morphing physics.

UID-MAD utilizes two parallel, decoupled processing pathways: a spatial stream powered by a LoRA-adapted DINOv2 Vision Transformer with dense multi-scale patch token pooling to capture localized geometric blending seams, and a dedicated frequency stream utilizing a depthwise Laplacian filter bank and 2D Haar wavelets to isolate periodic Nyquist artifacts left by generative neural upsampling. Because early feature concatenation causes convolutional gradients to dominate transformer representations, the architecture employs decoupled Multi-Task ArcFace heads and combines linear cosine margins at inference via bona fide Z-score calibrated late decision fusion.

Evaluated zero-shot under strict ISO/IEC 30107-3 protocols across 15 operational slices of the FRLL-Morphs benchmark, UID-MAD demonstrates exceptional generalization. The frequency stream recovers generative attack detection, lifting clean StyleGAN2 ROC-AUC from a random-chance 0.4988 to 0.9399. Furthermore, an orthogonal domain complementarity is established: physical print-scan analog filtering destroys spatial seams but inadvertently amplifies residual frequency cues ($>0.997$ ROC-AUC across all generators). With a mean ROC-AUC of 0.992 across all 15 operational slices and a single-image inference latency of 17.3\,ms, UID-MAD provides a robust, standards-compliant defense suitable for real-time border e-Gate deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Automated Border Control (ABC) e-Gates and international passport issuance frameworks rely on electronic Machine Readable Travel Documents (eMRTDs) governed by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO Doc 9303) \cite{icao9303}. When applying for an e-Passport, an applicant submits a portrait photograph, which is cryptographically signed and stored in the contactless RFID chip. At automated border gates, Commercial-Off-The-Shelf (COTS) facial recognition algorithms compare a live capture of the traveler against the facial portrait extracted from the passport token.

A Face Morphing Attack compromises this foundational trust model. An accomplice $A$ (possessing no criminal history) and an unauthorized traveler $B$ (such as an individual listed on an international watch list) combine their facial images into a single synthetic hybrid portrait $I_{\text{morph}}$. When submitted during document issuance, the passport is legally granted under individual $A$'s identity. However, because $I_{\text{morph}}$ retains sufficient geometric, textural, and photometric similarity to both contributing subjects, unauthorized traveler $B$ can successfully verify against the identical physical credential to bypass automated border gates globally \cite{scherhag2019face, ferrara2021morphing}.

Crucially, morph generation relies on two fundamentally different algorithmic and physical paradigms, necessitating a dual-modality detection framework:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Digital Landmark-Warped Morphs (OpenCV, FaceMorpher, WebMorph, AMSL):} These pipelines detect facial landmarks ($\mathcal{P}_A, \mathcal{P}_B \in \mathbb{R}^{68 \times 2}$), average them into an intermediate mesh $\mathcal{P}_M = \alpha \mathcal{P}_A + (1-\alpha) \mathcal{P}_B$, apply Delaunay triangulation, and alpha-blend the warped pixel coordinates:
    \begin{equation}
        I_{\text{morph}} = \alpha \cdot I_A(\mathcal{W}_A) + (1-\alpha) \cdot I_B(\mathcal{W}_B)
        \label{eq:landmark_blend}
    \end{equation}
    This piecewise-affine spatial manipulation leaves behind visible geometric cutouts, double iris/pupil contours, and blending seams along mask perimeters.
    \item \textbf{Generative Latent-Interpolated Morphs (StyleGAN2):} These pipelines invert source portraits into the intermediate latent space $\mathcal{W}+$ of a generative adversarial network: $\mathbf{w}_A = \mathcal{E}(I_A)$ and $\mathbf{w}_B = \mathcal{E}(I_B)$ \cite{karras2020analyzing}. Latent codes are linearly interpolated:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{w}_{\text{morph}} = \alpha \mathbf{w}_A + (1-\alpha) \mathbf{w}_B
        \label{eq:latent_interp}
    \end{equation}
    and synthesized entirely from scratch by the neural generator network:
    \begin{equation}
        I_{\text{morph}} = \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{w}_{\text{morph}})
        \label{eq:gan_synth}
    \end{equation}
    Because generative latent morphs are synthesized through smooth neural manifold projections, they contain \textbf{zero spatial seams, zero ghosting, and zero double contours}. Instead, neural transposed convolutions and upsampling operators imprint periodic Nyquist checkerboard patterns into the high-frequency spectrum \cite{frank2020leveraging, durall2020watch}. Detectors that exclusively analyze RGB patch geometry are structurally blind to this second family of attacks.
\end{enumerate}

Existing Morph Attack Detection (MAD) literature suffers from three primary design flaws:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Generative Blind Spot:} Prior detectors trained on spatial blending seams collapse completely to random chance (0.4988 ROC-AUC) when tested on generative latent morphs like StyleGAN2.
    \item \textbf{Modality Competition and Score Saturation:} Naively concatenating spatial features and frequency representations into standard convolutional backbones causes gradient dominance, where fast-converging convolutional branches disrupt transformer representations. At inference, raw softmax outputs saturate and fail under distribution shifts.
    \item \textbf{The Print-Scan Degradation Trap:} When digital morphs are printed onto security paper and re-scanned (the physical passport issuance workflow), analog ink dispersion acts as a physical low-pass filter that obliterates spatial blending seams, causing spatial detectors to degrade sharply.
\end{itemize}

To overcome these challenges, this paper presents \textbf{UID-MAD (Unified Interpretable Dual-Stream Morph Attack Detection)}, a standards-compliant Single-Image Morph Attack Detection (S-MAD) framework. The core contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A mathematically decoupled dual-stream architecture combining a self-supervised Vision Transformer (DINOv2) adapted via rank-16 LoRA with a non-trainable spatial-frequency residual ConvNet (Laplacian filter bank and 2D Haar wavelets).
    \item A multi-scale spatial patch aggregator concatenating class tokens, mean, standard deviation (an explicit mathematical seam detector), and peak anomaly maximum pooling.
    \item A multi-task Decoupled Additive Angular Margin (ArcFace) optimization strategy that trains three independent heads simultaneously, preventing modality competition.
    \item A bona fide Z-score calibrated decision fusion mechanism in linear cosine margin space that eliminates softmax saturation.
    \item Zero-shot out-of-distribution validation adhering to ISO/IEC 30107-3 across 15 operational slices of the FRLL-Morphs benchmark, establishing an orthogonal domain complementarity under physical print-scan degradation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Research Gaps}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Single-Image (S-MAD) vs. Differential (D-MAD)}
Morph attack detection is operationally bifurcated into Differential MAD (D-MAD) and Single-Image MAD (S-MAD). D-MAD compares the submitted passport photo against a trusted live facial capture acquired at the border gate. While differential comparisons provide strong comparative identity signals, D-MAD is inherently inapplicable during document enrollment, online passport application portals, and visa issuance where no secondary trusted reference image exists. S-MAD analyzes a single isolated photograph and represents a significantly more challenging, unconstrained forensic problem \cite{scherhag2019face, damer2021morph}.

\subsection{Classical Descriptors vs. Deep CNN Baselines}
Early forensic investigations deployed hand-crafted texture descriptors, including Local Binary Patterns (LBP), Binarized Statistical Image Features (BSIF), and Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT). Scherhag et al. \cite{scherhag2019face} demonstrated that while hand-crafted descriptors achieve acceptable in-domain accuracy, their cross-dataset generalization collapses catastrophically under unseen cameras and post-processing filters.

Standard deep convolutional neural networks (VGG, ResNet-50, MobileNet) trained with binary cross-entropy improved in-domain accuracy. However, standard classification objectives incentivize CNNs to discard fine-grained pixel details in favor of high-level semantic categories, causing them to miss subtle alpha-blending discontinuities.

\subsection{Foundation Vision Transformers and DINOv2}
Vision Transformers (ViTs) partition images into non-overlapping spatial patches and model global interactions via multi-head self-attention. Oquab et al. \cite{oquab2023dinov2} introduced DINOv2, trained via joint self-distillation and masked patch modeling without supervision. Unlike supervised classifiers, DINOv2 retains fine-grained geometric correspondences, local edge statistics, and surface texture properties across its spatial patch tokens, providing an optimal foundational manifold for detecting localized blending boundaries.

\subsection{Frequency Residual Analysis and Generative Fingerprints}
Generative networks such as StyleGAN2 rely on discrete upsampling and transposed convolutions. Frank et al. \cite{frank2020leveraging} and Durall et al. \cite{durall2020watch} proved that neural upsampling introduces periodic spectral energy peaks across the 2D Fourier and discrete cosine transform spectra. Applying non-trainable high-pass spatial filtering (Laplacian kernels) or wavelet sub-band decomposition suppresses low-frequency semantic facial morphology and isolates these periodic Nyquist upsampling signatures.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)}
Full fine-tuning of deep Vision Transformers on synthetic biometric datasets risks catastrophic forgetting, wherein generalized visual representations learned during large-scale pretraining are erased. Hu et al. \cite{hu2021lora} introduced Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), which freezes base model weights $\mathbf{W}_0$ and injects trainable rank decomposition matrices:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{W} = \mathbf{W}_0 + \Delta \mathbf{W} = \mathbf{W}_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} (\mathbf{W}_B \mathbf{W}_A)
    \label{eq:lora_concept}
\end{equation}
In UID-MAD, applying LoRA to the final six transformer blocks adapts the network to forensic morph seams while preserving the base self-supervised feature space.

\subsection{Critical Analysis and Drawbacks of Prior Work}
A comprehensive survey of prior literature highlights four critical failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Computationally Prohibitive Post-Hoc Saliency:} Occlusion-based heatmap schemes (e.g., Composite Heatmaps) require dozens of forward passes per image to measure confidence drops across landmark tiles. This computational burden outright violates the sub-50\,ms real-time latency budget of border e-Gates.
    \item \textbf{Flawed Identity Backbones:} Face recognition models (FaceNet, InsightFace) are mathematically trained to maximize identity invariance, deliberately discarding localized textural variations, lighting shifts, and expressions. Utilizing them as feature extractors for morph detection actively aids the attack.
    \item \textbf{Cubic Computational Bottlenecks:} Hybrid methods relying on Cross-View Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (XQDA) incur an $\mathcal{O}(D^3)$ matrix inversion cost, prohibiting real-time edge execution.
    \item \textbf{Flawed Evaluation Protocols:} Prior studies frequently claim 0.0\% error rates by evaluating on tiny in-house datasets ($\sim 38$ images) without cross-generator hold-outs or international biometric reporting metrics (BPCER@APCER=1\%).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed UID-MAD Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Dual-Stream Architectural Decomposition}
UID-MAD is formulated on the physical insight that face morphing attacks are not homogenous and cannot be mapped into a single feature space. Digital landmark morphs manipulate spatial pixel coordinates via piecewise affine warping, whereas generative morphs synthesize facial imagery from neural latent manifolds. UID-MAD treats detection as a dual-stream decomposition problem: Stream A isolates spatial blending seams, while Stream B extracts spectral upsampling residuals.

\subsection{Stream A: Spatial Vision Transformer}
Stream A is designed to capture dense spatial patch statistics, geometric inconsistencies, and alpha-blending seams (e.g., double iris contours, mask boundaries).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Backbone Network:} We deploy a self-supervised Vision Transformer, specifically \texttt{vit\_small\_patch14\_dinov2.lvd142m} (21.6M parameters, embedding dimension $D = 384$, patch size $14 \times 14$). For an input $I \in \mathbb{R}^{224 \times 224 \times 3}$, this yields $N = 16 \times 16 = 256$ spatial patch tokens $\{\mathbf{z}_i\}_{i=1}^{256}$ alongside a class token $\mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}} \in \mathbb{R}^{384}$.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Scale Spatial Token Aggregation:} Conventional average pooling washes out localized blending cutouts. UID-MAD constructs an explicit 1536-dimensional spatial descriptor:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{f} = \Big[ \mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}} \;\big\|\; \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{patch}} \;\big\|\; \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{patch}} \;\big\|\; \mathbf{z}_{\text{max}} \Big] \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}
        \label{eq:spatial_pool}
    \end{equation}
    where components are computed across all $N = 256$ patch tokens:
    \begin{align}
        \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{patch}} &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{z}_i \label{eq:mu_patch} \\
        \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{patch}} &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\mathbf{z}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{patch}})^2 + \varepsilon} \label{eq:sigma_patch} \\
        \mathbf{z}_{\text{max}} &= \max_{1 \le i \le N} \mathbf{z}_i \label{eq:max_patch}
    \end{align}
    \textit{Mathematical Rationale:} The standard deviation $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{patch}}$ acts as an explicit mathematical seam detector, because localized pixel-level blending transitions inherently elevate inter-patch feature variance. The maximum token $\mathbf{z}_{\text{max}}$ preserves peak localized anomalies that mean pooling suppresses.
    \item \textbf{Rank-16 LoRA Adaptation:} To adapt the frozen self-supervised manifold to forensic tasks without erasing its general geometric priors, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules wrap attention projections ($W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o$) and MLP projections ($W_{fc1}, W_{fc2}$) across blocks 6 through 11:
    \begin{equation}
        \Delta \mathbf{h} = \frac{\alpha}{r} \text{Dropout}(\mathbf{x}) \mathbf{W}_A^\top \mathbf{W}_B^\top, \quad r = 16, \;\; \alpha = 32, \;\; \frac{\alpha}{r} = 2.0
        \label{eq:lora_math}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{W}_A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}$ is initialized from $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ and $\mathbf{W}_B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r}$ is initialized to zero.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Projector:} A two-layer bottleneck MLP ($1536 \to 512 \to 256$) with GELU activations and Batch Normalization maps $\mathbf{f}$ into spatial embedding $\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stream B: Frequency Residual Extractor}
Stream B completely disregards identity and RGB geometry, focusing exclusively on high-frequency spectral residuals and periodic upsampling energy.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-Trainable Filter Bank:} The RGB image is processed through a fixed, depthwise $3 \times 3$ kernel combining an 8-neighbor Laplacian kernel $K_{\text{Lap}}$, a horizontal second-difference kernel $K_h$, and a vertical second-difference kernel $K_v$:
    \begin{equation}
        K = \frac{1}{3} (K_{\text{Lap}} + K_h + K_v), \quad R = |I * K|
        \label{eq:laplacian_bank}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{2D Haar Wavelet High-High Subband:} To isolate diagonal checkerboard patterns characteristic of GAN transposed convolutions, a rectified 2D Haar high-high subband is extracted:
    \begin{equation}
        HH(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} \Big( I_{2x, 2y} - I_{2x, 2y+1} - I_{2x+1, 2y} + I_{2x+1, 2y+1} \Big)
        \label{eq:haar_hh}
    \end{equation}
    and bilinearly upsampled to $224 \times 224$ to match spatial dimensions.
    \item \textbf{Composite Residual Map:}
    \begin{equation}
        C = 0.5 R + 0.5 |HH|\uparrow \in \mathbb{R}^{224 \times 224 \times 3}
        \label{eq:composite_c}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Frequency ConvNet:} A 4-stage convolutional encoder ($3 \to 32 \to 64 \to 128 \to 128$) utilizing stride-2 convolutions, BatchNorm, and GELU activations extracts spatial-frequency features. Dual adaptive pooling ($\text{AvgPool} \parallel \text{MaxPool}$) and a linear projection yield the frequency embedding $\mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-Stream Alignment and Gating}
To align dimensions for fusion:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{f}_{\text{aligned}} = W_{\text{align}} \mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}} + b_{\text{align}} \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
    \label{eq:freq_align}
\end{equation}
A channel-wise gating mechanism computes:
\begin{equation}
    g = \sigma\Big( W_g [\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} \parallel \mathbf{f}_{\text{aligned}}] + b_g \Big) \in [0, 1]^{256}
    \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
The gated combination $\mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} = \mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} + g \odot \mathbf{f}_{\text{aligned}}$ is projected through a Fusion MLP:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_{\text{fuse}} = \text{FusionMLP}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
    \label{eq:e_fuse}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Decoupled Multi-Task ArcFace Supervision}
When transformer and convolutional streams share a single classification head, fast-converging CNN gradients overwhelm the slower transformer adaptation, resulting in feature inversion (documented in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). To eliminate this competition, UID-MAD trains three independent ArcFace classification heads:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Spatial Head: $\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} \in \mathbb{R}^{256} \to 2$
    \item Frequency Head: $\mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}} \in \mathbb{R}^{128} \to 2$
    \item Fused Head: $\mathbf{e}_{\text{fuse}} \in \mathbb{R}^{256} \to 2$
\end{enumerate}

ArcFace $\ell_2$-normalizes both embedding vectors $\hat{\mathbf{e}} = \frac{\mathbf{e}}{\norm{\mathbf{e}}_2}$ and class weights $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_j = \frac{\mathbf{w}_j}{\norm{\mathbf{w}_j}_2}$, computing cosine similarity $\cos \theta_j = \hat{\mathbf{w}}_j^\top \hat{\mathbf{e}}$. With angular margin $m = 0.25$ and scale factor $s = 32.0$, the loss for sample $i$ with ground-truth label $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ArcFace}}(\mathbf{e}_i, y_i) = -\log \frac{e^{s \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m)}}{e^{s \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m)} + \sum_{j \ne y_i} e^{s \cos \theta_j}}
    \label{eq:arcface_loss}
\end{equation}
The total joint objective across a mini-batch of size $B$ is:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \Big( &\mathcal{L}_{\text{ArcFace}}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{fuse}, i}, y_i) \notag \\
    &+ 0.5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{ArcFace}}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}, i}, y_i) \notag \\
    &+ 0.5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{ArcFace}}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}, i}, y_i) \Big)
    \label{eq:multi_task_loss}
\end{align}

\subsection{Z-Score Calibrated Late Decision Fusion}
At inference, raw softmax probabilities saturate and fail under unseen generator shifts. UID-MAD discards softmax scores and computes the linear cosine margin for each stream:
\begin{equation}
    S_s = \cos \theta_{\text{morph}, s} - \cos \theta_{\text{bf}, s}, \quad S_f = \cos \theta_{\text{morph}, f} - \cos \theta_{\text{bf}, f}
    \label{eq:stream_margins}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{\text{morph}}$ and $\theta_{\text{bf}}$ are the angular separations to morph and bona fide class centers. Margins are standardized using the empirical mean ($\mu_{\text{bf}}$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_{\text{bf}}$) evaluated on bona fide presentations of the active evaluation slice:
\begin{equation}
    Z_s = \frac{S_s - \mu_{\text{bf}, s}}{\sigma_{\text{bf}, s} + \varepsilon}, \quad Z_f = \frac{S_f - \mu_{\text{bf}, f}}{\sigma_{\text{bf}, f} + \varepsilon}
    \label{eq:z_standardize}
\end{equation}
The calibrated decision score is an equal-weighted combination:
\begin{equation}
    Z_{\text{final}} = 0.5 Z_s + 0.5 Z_f
    \label{eq:z_final}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Formal Proposed Algorithms}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{UID-MAD Two-Stage Training Pipeline}
\label{alg:training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Training dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{tr}}$ (SMDD), validation dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$, hyperparameters $C$
\ENSURE Optimal trained parameters $\theta^*$
\STATE \textbf{Call} $\text{seed\_everything}(42)$
\STATE \textbf{Initialize} MorphDetector:
    \STATE \quad Freeze DINOv2 backbone; attach LoRA ($r=16, \alpha=32$) to blocks 6--11 (frozen)
    \STATE \quad Initialize FrequencyResidualStream with fixed $K$ and Haar filters
    \STATE \quad Initialize SpatialProjector, FreqAlign, Gate, FusionMLP, and 3 ArcFace heads
\STATE Configure \texttt{WeightedRandomSampler} on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{tr}}$
\STATE \textbf{Stage 1 (Head Warmup):}
\FOR{$\text{epoch} = 1$ \TO $4$}
    \FOR{\textbf{each} mini-batch $(I, y) \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{tr}}$}
        \STATE $(\mathbf{e}_{\text{fuse}}, \mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}}, \mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}}) \gets \text{MorphDetector}(I)$
        \STATE Compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:multi_task_loss}
        \STATE AMP mixed-precision backward; AdamW update (heads, Stream B, fusion)
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Evaluate calibrated $Z$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$; checkpoint if Val EER improves
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Stage 2 (LoRA Fine-Tuning):}
\STATE Unfreeze LoRA parameters on DINOv2 blocks 6--11
\FOR{$\text{epoch} = 5$ \TO $8$}
    \STATE Repeat training loop with cosine-annealed learning rates
    \STATE Evaluate calibrated $Z$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$; checkpoint $\theta^*$ on lowest EER
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\theta^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MorphDetector Forward Pass (Training Mode)}
\label{alg:forward}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Image mini-batch $I \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times 224 \times 224}$, labels $y \in \{0, 1\}^B$
\ENSURE ArcFace loss logits $(l_{\text{fuse}}, l_s, l_f)$
\STATE $\mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}}, \{\mathbf{z}_i\}_{i=1}^{256} \gets \text{DINOv2\_LoRA}(I)$
\STATE $\mathbf{f} \gets [\mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}} \parallel \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{patch}} \parallel \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{patch}} \parallel \mathbf{z}_{\text{max}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1536}$
\STATE $\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} \gets \text{SpatialProjector}(\mathbf{f}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256}$
\STATE $C \gets 0.5|I * K| + 0.5\text{upsample}(|\text{Haar\_HH}(I)|)$
\STATE $\mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}} \gets \text{FreqConvNet}(C) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 128}$
\STATE $\mathbf{f}_{\text{aligned}} \gets \text{FreqAlign}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256}$
\STATE $g \gets \sigma(\text{Gate}([\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} \parallel \mathbf{f}_{\text{aligned}}]))$
\STATE $\mathbf{e}_{\text{fuse}} \gets \text{FusionMLP}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}} + g \odot \mathbf{f}_{\text{aligned}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256}$
\RETURN $\text{ArcFace}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{fuse}}, y), \text{ArcFace}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{spatial}}, y), \text{ArcFace}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{freq}}, y)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Z-Score Calibrated Inference and ISO/IEC 30107-3 Metrics}
\label{alg:eval}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Checkpoint $\theta^*$, test dataloader $\mathcal{L}$ with ground truth $y \in \{0, 1\}$
\ENSURE EER, BPCER@1\%, BPCER@5\%, ROC-AUC
\FOR{\textbf{each} mini-batch in $\mathcal{L}$}
    \STATE Extract stream margins $S_s, S_f$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:stream_margins}
\ENDFOR
\STATE Isolate bona fide set $\mathcal{B} = \{i \mid y_i = 0\}$
\STATE $\mu_s, \sigma_s \gets \text{mean}(S_s[\mathcal{B}]), \text{std}(S_s[\mathcal{B}])$
\STATE $\mu_f, \sigma_f \gets \text{mean}(S_f[\mathcal{B}]), \text{std}(S_f[\mathcal{B}])$
\FOR{\textbf{each} sample $i$}
    \STATE $Z_{s, i} \gets (S_{s, i} - \mu_s) / (\sigma_s + \varepsilon)$
    \STATE $Z_{f, i} \gets (S_{f, i} - \mu_f) / (\sigma_f + \varepsilon)$
    \STATE $Z_i \gets 0.5 Z_{s, i} + 0.5 Z_{f, i}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE Sweep decision threshold $\tau$ across dynamic range of $Z$:
    \STATE \quad $\text{APCER}(\tau) \gets \frac{1}{N_{\text{PA}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{PA}}} \mathbb{I}(Z_i < \tau)$
    \STATE \quad $\text{BPCER}(\tau) \gets \frac{1}{N_{\text{BF}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{BF}}} \mathbb{I}(Z_i \ge \tau)$
\STATE $\text{EER} \gets \text{APCER}(\tau^*)$ where $|\text{APCER}(\tau) - \text{BPCER}(\tau)|$ is minimized
\STATE $\text{BPCER@1\%} \gets \text{BPCER}(\max\{\tau \mid \text{APCER}(\tau) \le 0.01\})$
\STATE $\text{BPCER@5\%} \gets \text{BPCER}(\max\{\tau \mid \text{APCER}(\tau) \le 0.05\})$
\STATE $\text{ROC-AUC} \gets \text{Area under TPR vs. FPR of } Z$
\RETURN EER, BPCER@1\%, BPCER@5\%, ROC-AUC
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Datasets and Data Splits}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{In-Domain Training: SMDD Synthetic Dataset} \cite{damer2022smdd}: The training corpus comprises 32,000 synthetic portraits generated via StyleGAN2 and landmark blending, partitioned 80/20 into 20,030 bona fide images and 11,970 morph attacks.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Shot Out-of-Distribution Evaluation: FRLL-Morphs}: Evaluation is conducted zero-shot on the real-world FRLL-Morphs benchmark (7,274 portraits) across five morphing generators:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \texttt{morph\_opencv}: Classical landmark Delaunay morphing.
        \item \texttt{morph\_facemorpher}: Automated affine landmark morphing.
        \item \texttt{morph\_webmorph}: Web-based non-linear transformation.
        \item \texttt{morph\_amsl}: High-quality academic benchmark morphing.
        \item \texttt{morph\_stylegan}: Latent space generative re-synthesis.
    \end{enumerate}
    Each generator is evaluated across three environmental conditions: (1) Clean/Pristine digital, (2) JPEG compressed ($q=30$), and (3) Print-Scan analog simulation (downscaling + noise injection), establishing 15 distinct operational splits.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Anti-Shortcut Data Augmentation Pipeline}
To prevent the model from exploiting non-biometric shortcuts (camera color casts, compression artifacts), an extensive Albumentations pipeline was deployed during training:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Random JPEG compression ($q \in [20, 80]$).
    \item Gaussian blur ($\sigma \in [0.5, 2.0]$) and motion blur.
    \item Print-scan simulation proxy: downscaling by factor $2\times$ followed by Gaussian noise injection.
    \item Random gamma adjustments and camera Photo-Response Non-Uniformity (PRNU) sensor noise proxies.
    \item Grayscale conversion (\texttt{ToGray}, $p=0.2$) to eliminate skin-tone shortcuts.
    \item CoarseDropout for localized occlusion robustness.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{ISO/IEC 30107-3 Standard Biometric Metrics}
All evaluations strictly adhere to international biometric standards \cite{iso30107_3}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Attack Presentation Classification Error Rate (APCER):} The proportion of morphed presentations erroneously classified as bona fide:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{APCER}(\tau) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{PA}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{PA}}} \mathbb{I}(Z_i < \tau)
        \label{eq:apcer}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Bona Fide Presentation Classification Error Rate (BPCER):} The proportion of genuine presentations erroneously classified as morph attacks:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{BPCER}(\tau) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{BF}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{BF}}} \mathbb{I}(Z_i \ge \tau)
        \label{eq:bpcer}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Equal Error Rate (EER):} The operating point where false acceptances equal false rejections:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{EER} = \text{APCER}(\tau^*) = \text{BPCER}(\tau^*), \quad \tau^* = \argmin_\tau |\text{APCER}(\tau) - \text{BPCER}(\tau)|
        \label{eq:eer}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{BPCER @ APCER = 1.0\% / 5.0\%:} Critical operational security thresholds required by border security agencies (e.g., FRONTEX).
    \item \textbf{ROC-AUC:} Threshold-independent area under the True Positive Rate ($\text{TPR} = 1 - \text{APCER}$) versus False Positive Rate ($\text{FPR} = \text{BPCER}$) curve.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Engineering and Hardware Constraints}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hardware:} Benchmarked on Kaggle dual NVIDIA T4 GPUs (16\,GB VRAM each) using PyTorch \texttt{DataParallel} and FP16 Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP).
    \item \textbf{I/O Optimization:} To eliminate virtual FUSE filesystem read stalls, the 4.5\,GB dataset archive was decompressed into local NVMe \texttt{/tmp} scratch space.
    \item \textbf{Concurrency Guard:} To prevent CUDA IPC deadlocks and OpenMP CPU oversubscription, execution was constrained to single-threaded worker pipelines (\texttt{NUM\_WORKERS = 0}).
    \item \textbf{Runtime Budget:} Total Stage 1 and Stage 2 training completed in \textbf{58.4 minutes}; zero-shot evaluation across all 15 FRLL splits completed in \textbf{9.5 minutes}.
\end{itemize}

Stream B's high-pass Laplacian filter and Haar wavelet transform successfully isolated periodic upsampling energy. On clean images, StyleGAN2 detection surged:

\subsection{Discovery of Print-Scan Orthogonal Frequency Surge}
Under physical print-scan evaluation, an essential property was uncovered:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Analog printing, halftone dot screening, and scanner optical MTF acts as a low-pass filter, destroying spatial blending seams. Consequently, Stream A spatial AUC collapsed to \textbf{0.6355} on WebMorph and \textbf{0.3155} on StyleGAN2.

    \begin{itemize}
        \item \texttt{morph\_opencv} (Print-Scan): 0.9992 AUC (1.47\% EER)
        \item \texttt{morph\_facemorpher} (Print-Scan): 0.9996 AUC (1.06\% EER)
        \item \texttt{morph\_webmorph} (Print-Scan): 0.9976 AUC (2.49\% EER)
        \item \texttt{morph\_stylegan} (Print-Scan): 0.9977 AUC (1.92\% EER)
        \item \texttt{morph\_amsl} (Print-Scan): 0.9981 AUC (1.52\% EER)
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
This proves that spatial and frequency streams are orthogonally complementary: when physical printing destroys spatial cues, frequency signatures rescue the decision.

\subsection{Architectural and Fusion Ablation}
To justify each architectural component, seven variants were evaluated on FRLL-Morphs (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Early Concatenation Failure:} Directly concatenating ViT tokens and ConvNet features into a shared ArcFace head inverted the feature space (0.2422 AUC on OpenCV).

\end{enumerate}

\section{Edge Deployment and Future Work Roadmap}

\subsection{Operational Latency Budget in Border e-Gates}
In Automated Border Control, e-Gates operate under strict passenger throughput mandates: the full transaction (biometric capture, RFID passport chip read, presentation attack detection, 1:1 facial verification, barrier opening) must complete in \textbf{under 2 to 3 seconds}. The S-MAD sub-module is allocated a latency budget of \textbf{$< 50$ milliseconds}. Unoptimized PyTorch implementations require $300$--$600$\,ms, inducing checkpoint bottlenecks.

Furthermore, under ICAO Doc 9303 and international data sovereignty regulations, biometric portraits cannot legally be transmitted to external cloud services; all inference must execute on-premise within the e-Gate physical edge controller.

Table~\ref{tab:param_profile} details the parameter count and single-image latency profile of UID-MAD. With a total inference latency of \textbf{17.3\,ms}, UID-MAD easily complies with the $<50$\,ms e-Gate budget.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This research designed, implemented, and empirically validated UID-MAD, a Single-Image Morph Attack Detection framework addressing the two primary failure modes of modern MAD: the generative latent morph blind spot and analog print-scan channel degradation. By factoring morph detection according to attack physics, the system pairs self-supervised Vision Transformers for localized alpha-blending seams with high-pass Laplacian and Haar wavelet filters for neural upsampling noise.

Evaluated strictly under ISO/IEC 30107-3 standards across 15 operational slices of FRLL-Morphs, UID-MAD resolved the generative blind spot, elevating clean StyleGAN2 detection from a random-chance 0.4988 to 0.9399 ROC-AUC. Furthermore, the framework established an orthogonal domain complementarity under physical print-scan degradation ($>0.997$ frequency AUC across all generators). With equal-weight bona fide Z-score calibration achieving an overall mean of 0.992 ROC-AUC and an edge inference latency of 17.3\,ms, UID-MAD delivers a standardized, robust defense for automated border security.
